# Video Script Structure

When creating actual video scripts, follow this proven sequence. Every script must hit each stage in order:

**Pattern Interrupt → Hook → (Framing) Why It Matters → Curiosity Loop → Escalation → Payoff → Relevance Bridge → Optional New Loop**

### Pre-Script Requirements

Before writing the script stages, address these two critical elements:

#### 1. Address Big TAM (Total Addressable Market)

Whatever you're talking about — even if it's niche — must address a much bigger audience by expanding the importance from a small context to a universal one. Never lead with niche terminology or feature names. Instead, start from a problem or desire that millions of people share, then narrow into your specific solution.

**Example — WRONG approach** (small TAM, niche-first):
> "Today I'll show you the advanced settings in [Niche Tool] and how they work."

**Example — RIGHT approach** (big TAM, universal problem first):
> "You know how you can spend hours organizing your ideas and still feel like you're going in circles?
> Most tools make this worse, not better.
> That's because they treat ideas as isolated items — not as connected concepts.
> What if there was a way to see the hidden connections between everything you're working on?
> [Tool] does exactly that — it maps your thinking so you can find the gaps and breakthroughs.
> If you research, create, or make decisions — this is the difference between surface-level and real depth."

The principle: Start with the big audience problem, then introduce your specific solution. The viewer should feel "this is for me" before they even know what tool or method you're demonstrating.

#### 2. Validate Your Credibility

Early in the video (typically right after the hook or during the framing stage), establish why the viewer should trust you. Briefly mention:
- Who you are
- What you're working on / have built / have experienced
- Why you have authority on this topic

Keep it short and natural — not a resume, but a trust signal. Examples:
- "I've been [doing X] for [Y years]..."
- "I built [product/business] that [result]..."
- "After [impressive experience]..."

### Script Stages

#### Stage 1: Pattern Interrupt

Before the hook even lands, something must visually or cognitively interrupt scrolling. This is the first 1-3 seconds.

**Techniques:**
- **Unexpected statement**: "What if I told you that [surprising claim]?"
- **Strong claim**: "Everyone talks about [popular thing] but no one mentions [hidden problem]"
- **Visual contrast**: Quick switch of one frame to another (e.g., chaos → clarity)
- **Fast pacing**: Rapid cuts or motion in first frames
- **Direct address**: "If you care about [topic]..."
- **Pattern violation**: "Stop [common behavior] — do this instead"
- **Identity challenge**: "If you think you know [topic], you don't"

**Alignment rule**: The pattern interrupt should align with your channel's positioning. If your positioning is "practical results, not theory," then your interrupt should feel like hype → reality.

#### Stage 2: Hook (Promise of Value)

The hook answers: **"Why should I care?"**

For big TAM content, this often signals:
- Relevance to their life/work
- Strategic advantage
- Fast results
- Hidden knowledge

**Examples:**
- "Here's what's really driving [industry/trend]."
- "This is how you spot [problem] before everyone else."
- "Understand any [complex topic] in under a minute."
- "This is the hidden structure behind [visible phenomenon]."

#### Stage 3: Framing (Why This Matters)

Many creators skip this — that's a mistake. You need to answer: **"Why does this affect ME?"**

This is where you expand the TAM impact and make it personal to the viewer.

**Examples:**
- "If you're [building/learning/creating/investing] in [area], this changes how you think."
- "This approach will dramatically improve [specific outcome the viewer wants]."
- "Whether you're a [role A], [role B], or [role C] — this is the difference between [bad state] and [good state]."

**This is also where credibility validation fits naturally** — after establishing why it matters, briefly mention why you're the one to show them.

#### Stage 4: Curiosity Loop

Open a knowledge gap — create a question in the viewer's mind that they need answered.

The curiosity loop works by revealing that something hidden exists without yet explaining what it is.

**Examples:**
- "There is a structural reason [common problem keeps happening]."
- "Most people [do common thing] wrong — and there's proof for why."
- "The best [practitioners] don't [do thing] the way you think they do."

#### Stage 5: Escalation (Deepen the Stakes)

Increase the perceived importance. Add more tension. Make the viewer feel this is bigger than they thought.

**Examples:**
- "And it's actually the same reason we have so much [bigger problem] in the world."
- "This isn't just about [niche topic] — it's about how [universal system] works too."
- "The longer you ignore this, the more [negative consequence] becomes."

#### Stage 6: Payoff (Insight Delivery)

Resolve the curiosity loop. Deliver the core insight clearly and memorably.

**Examples:**
- "When you [analyze/map/test] it, the pattern becomes obvious — [insight]."
- "The [method/framework] reveals what's already been said — and more importantly, what hasn't."
- "It turns out the gap between [concept A] and [concept B] is where the [desired outcome] lives."

#### Stage 7: Relevance Bridge / Implication (Optional but Powerful)

After the payoff, connect it back to the viewer: **"Here's what this means for you."**

This is where strategy happens — turning insight into action.

**Examples:**
- "So if you want [outcome], you need to [specific action]. And this is exactly what [tool/method] does."
- "This means every time you [common behavior] without this step, you're getting the same [bad result] as everyone else."
- "Now you can see why just [common approach] doesn't work — you need to change [specific thing]."

#### Stage 8: Loop Reopen (Optional)

For retention, series creation, or looping the video back to the beginning:
- Tease the next question that emerges from the payoff
- Open a new curiosity gap that either loops the video or leads to another video
- "But here's the thing nobody's talking about yet..."
- "Now the question becomes: what happens when you combine this with [next topic]?"

### Script Template

```
[PATTERN INTERRUPT — 1-3 seconds]
[Strong visual or verbal disruption aligned with channel positioning]

[HOOK — next 5-7 seconds]
[Promise of value: Why should they care?]

[FRAMING — next 10-15 seconds]
[Why this matters to THEM + credibility validation]

[CURIOSITY LOOP — next 10-15 seconds]
[Open the knowledge gap — reveal something hidden exists]

[ESCALATION — next 10-15 seconds]
[Deepen the stakes — make it bigger than expected]

[PAYOFF — next 15-30 seconds]
[Deliver the core insight, resolve the loop]

[RELEVANCE BRIDGE — next 10-15 seconds]
[What this means for the viewer — actionable implication]

[LOOP REOPEN — final 5-10 seconds] (optional)
[New question or tease for next video / video loop]
```

### Script Checklist

Before finalizing any script, verify:
- [ ] **Big TAM**: Does the opening address a universal problem before going niche?
- [ ] **Credibility**: Is there an early trust signal about who you are / what you've built?
- [ ] **Pattern Interrupt**: Will the first 1-3 seconds stop a scroller?
- [ ] **Hook**: Is there a clear promise of value within 10 seconds?
- [ ] **Framing**: Does the viewer know why this matters to THEM specifically?
- [ ] **Curiosity Loop**: Is there a gap opened that demands to be closed?
- [ ] **Escalation**: Do the stakes feel higher than the viewer initially expected?
- [ ] **Payoff**: Is the insight clear, memorable, and satisfying?
- [ ] **Relevance Bridge**: Does the viewer know what to do with this information?
- [ ] **No niche jargon in the opening**: Feature names and technical terms come AFTER the universal framing
- [ ] **SEO-script alignment**: Do the script stages map to a real content gap found during SEO research?
